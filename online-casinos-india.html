How It Works

India's gambling law used to be a patchwork. The central Public Gambling Act of 1867 - written long before the internet - prohibited running common gaming houses for games of chance, while individual states set their own rules: Sikkim and Nagaland created licensing regimes, Goa and Sikkim allowed land-based casinos, and states like Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu banned online gaming outright. Offshore casinos operated in the resulting grey area, and a 28% GST on real-money gaming (from October 2023) even suggested the sector was being normalized rather than outlawed.

That changed decisively in 2025. Citing addiction, financial ruin and money laundering, the central government pushed through the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025: passed by the Lok Sabha on 20 August and the Rajya Sabha on 21 August, with presidential assent on 22 August 2025. On 22 April 2026 MeitY notified enforcement from 1 May 2026, together with the Online Gaming Rules, 2026 and the constitution of the Online Gaming Authority of India. The Act is deliberately sweeping: it covers every "online money game" - skill, chance or hybrid - plus their advertisement, promotion and financial facilitation. Esports and purely social games are exempt and even promoted under the same law.

Enforcement has been aggressive. MeitY told the Lok Sabha that 8,376 betting and gambling URLs had been blocked or actioned by March 2026, and the Enforcement Directorate has pursued offshore operators (around ₹150 crore frozen or seized in the Parimatch case, per ED accounts). Major domestic platforms - Dream11, MPL, PokerBaazi, WinZO, Zupee - shut or suspended real-money operations within days of the bill passing in August 2025. Several companies challenged the law in high courts (arguing it wrongly criminalizes games of skill), and the cases are being consolidated for a Supreme Court hearing - but no stay has been granted, so the ban stands in full force as of September 2026.

For individual players, the practical message is unambiguous: there is currently no legal online casino option in India, domestic or offshore. Offshore sites still advertise to Indian users, but using them means transacting with operators the Indian state is actively blocking and prosecuting. Treat any site claiming to be "legal in India" with extreme skepticism.

Payment Methods

Payments are where the ban bites hardest. Under the Act, banks and financial institutions are barred from transferring funds for online money games - facilitating such transactions is itself an offence punishable by up to 3 years in prison and/or a ₹1 crore fine, and MeitY has told banks their compliance role is spelled out in the framework. Around 80% of real-money gaming transactions in India historically flowed through UPI; since the ban, UPI gaming transaction volumes fell sharply (NPCI data showed a ~25% month-on-month drop in August 2025 alone). MeitY has directed banks and payment intermediaries to block transactions linked to online money games.

Crypto is not a legal workaround. India's regulatory stance on crypto remains restrictive, and routing gambling deposits through crypto does not change the underlying illegality of the game itself - it only adds unregulated-payment risk on top. If a casino's cashier cannot process your UPI deposit, that is the law working as intended, not a technical glitch.

What Remains Legal

🏇 Horse Racing

Betting on horse racing at licensed turf clubs is regulated separately and was historically treated as a game of skill by Indian courts. It is not an "online money game" under the 2025 Act's framework in the way casino games are.

🎟️ State Lotteries

Lotteries run by state governments under the Lotteries (Regulation) Act, 1998 remain legal where the state permits them. Only authorized state lotteries - not private online "lottery" sites.

🎮 Esports & Social Games

The 2025 Act explicitly exempts and promotes esports and online social games (no stakes, no monetary winnings). Competitive gaming without money on the line is unaffected by the ban.

FAQ

Is online gambling legal in India?

No - not since 1 May 2026. The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 bans all online money games nationwide, whether skill- or chance-based. Before that, the position varied by state; the central law has now overridden the patchwork for online play.

What is the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025?

Act No. 32 of 2025, passed 20-21 August 2025 (assent 22 August 2025) and in force from 1 May 2026 with the Online Gaming Rules, 2026. It prohibits online money games, their advertising and promotion, and financial transactions connected with them, while exempting and promoting esports and social games. An Online Gaming Authority of India was constituted to administer it.

What are the penalties?

Offering or facilitating an online money game: up to 3 years in prison and/or a ₹1 crore fine (3-5 years and ₹1-2 crore for repeat offences). Advertising or promoting one: up to 2 years and/or a ₹50 lakh fine. Banks and payment firms that process related transactions also face liability.

Can I still play at offshore casinos from India?

No - legally, you cannot. Offshore operation does not exempt a site: the Act targets the activity regardless of where the operator sits, MeitY is actively blocking offshore gambling URLs (8,376 blocked as of March 2026), and the Enforcement Directorate is pursuing offshore operators. Any site still accepting Indian players is operating against Indian law.

Why won't my UPI deposit go through at a casino?

Banks and payment systems are legally barred from processing online money-game transactions, and UPI rails for unlicensed gaming merchants are being blocked. This is enforcement of the ban, not a payment error - and crypto does not make the underlying activity legal.

What was the law before 2026?

The 1867 Public Gambling Act plus state-level rules: a grey area where offshore casinos served Indian players while states like Telangana and Tamil Nadu banned online gaming and Sikkim/Nagaland licensed some formats. A 28% GST applied to real-money gaming from October 2023. The 2025 Act replaced this patchwork with a nationwide prohibition for online money games.

Could the ban be overturned?

Constitutional challenges (notably on whether banning games of skill is valid) are pending and being consolidated before the Supreme Court, with a final hearing awaited. However, no court has granted a stay - the law is fully in force as of September 2026. This page will be updated if the legal position changes.

What is the legal gambling age in India?

Generally 18+, though it varies by state and format (land-based casinos in Goa and Sikkim, lotteries and racing have their own age rules). Regardless of age, online money games are currently prohibited for everyone.
