How to Check If an Online Casino Is Safe in Kenya (2026)

By Spinora Wins Editorial Team | Last updated: September 2026

  1. Home
  2. Kenya
  3. Safety Checklist
TL;DR: Kenya makes verification straightforward: check the operator on the official licensed list, finish KYC (selfie plus national ID) before depositing, read the withdrawal terms, and walk away from any site that is not on the register.

Step 1: Check the Licence on the Official Register

Kenya is in a regulatory handover: the Gambling Control Act, 2025 replaced the Betting Control and Licensing Board (BCLB) with the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Kenya (GRA). During the transition, existing BCLB licences remain valid until expiry, and new licensing runs under the GRA framework.

  • Find the regulator's official list of licensed operators.
  • Search for the exact domain you plan to use.
  • Check the licence is current, not expired or suspended.

Do this before creating an account, not after depositing. Two minutes of checking is the strongest protection a Kenyan player has.

Step 2: Ignore Logos, Trust the Register

Unlicensed sites routinely display copied regulator logos and claim "licensed in Kenya" in their footer. A logo is just an image. Only the official register counts. Kenya enforces this: in 2025 the BCLB announced the shutdown of 58 illegal gambling platforms.

Step 3: Complete KYC Before Depositing

Licensed Kenyan operators must verify your identity. New online bettors are required to submit a selfie photo with their national ID, and you will verify the M-Pesa number on your account. Finish KYC before your first deposit, not when you try to withdraw - that is when unverified accounts get stuck.

Step 4: Read the Terms, Especially Withdrawals

Before depositing, confirm:

  • Withdrawal rules, timeframes and limits in the cashier.
  • Bonus wagering requirements - see our bonuses guide.
  • How the 20% withholding tax on winnings is applied at payout.
  • Account dormancy and closure rules.

Step 5: Check Responsible-Gambling Tools

The 2025 Act requires consumer protection measures. Before depositing, confirm the casino offers deposit limits, session reminders and self-exclusion. If none exist, do not play there.

Red Flags: Walk Away Immediately

  • Not on the official licensed list. The big one.
  • Pressure to deposit via crypto, gift cards or P2P. Licensed Kenyan casinos use M-Pesa and banks.
  • No KYC. Zero verification means zero compliance.
  • Hidden bonus terms. If support cannot explain wagering, leave.
  • VPN pitch. If a site tells you to use a VPN to access it from Kenya, it is unlicensed.

If Something Goes Wrong

At a licensed casino: complain to the operator in writing, keep records, then escalate to the regulator if unresolved. At an unlicensed casino: you have no Kenyan authority to turn to. That is the real cost of playing offshore, and the main reason to stick to licensed operators. Our complaints guide covers the process in detail.

FAQ

How do I verify a casino is licensed in Kenya?

Check the regulator's register: under the Gambling Control Act 2025, licensing moved from the BCLB to the new Gambling Regulatory Authority of Kenya (GRA). Only play at operators that appear on the official licensed list with a current licence.

What KYC will a Kenyan casino ask for?

Expect ID verification: new online bettors at licensed operators must submit a selfie photo with their national ID. You will also verify your M-Pesa phone number. A casino that lets you deposit and withdraw large amounts with zero verification is not compliant.

What are red flags of an unsafe casino in Kenya?

No verifiable Kenyan licence, pressure to deposit via crypto or gift cards, bonus terms you cannot find, no responsible-gambling tools, and support that cannot explain withdrawal rules. Any one of these is enough to walk away.

Where can I complain about a licensed casino?

Complain to the operator in writing first and keep records. If it is unresolved, escalate to the regulator (GRA, formerly BCLB). Our complaints guide walks through the process step by step.

Is it safe to play at a casino licensed only in Curacao?

A Curacao licence is a real licence, but it is not a Kenyan licence: the Kenyan regulator cannot help you in a dispute. For Kenyan players, a local licence gives the strongest protection.
