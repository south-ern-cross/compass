How to Check If an Online Casino Is Safe for Mexican Players

By Spinora Wins Editorial Team | Last updated: September 2026

  1. Home
  2. Mexico
  3. Safety Checklist
TL;DR: Check five things before depositing: a verifiable license, a real company behind the site, clear withdrawal terms, working customer support, and a clean complaint record. Do KYC before you deposit, not after you win. If anything on this list fails, pick another casino.

Step 1: Verify the License

The license is the first filter. In Mexico's gray market, most sites serving Mexican players hold foreign licenses - that is normal. What is not normal is an unverifiable one.

  • Find it. Scroll to the casino's footer. A licensed operator names its regulator and usually shows a license number.
  • Check it. MGA licenses can be verified on the Malta Gaming Authority website register. Curacao licenses can be checked through the Gaming Control Board portal. Click the license seal - on legitimate sites it links to the regulator's verification page.
  • Walk away if: no license is mentioned anywhere, the number does not exist in the register, or the seal is a static image that links nowhere.

Read our legal guide for why Mexican players usually deal with foreign licenses.

Step 2: Identify the Company

A real casino is run by a real company. Look for:

  • A company name and registration jurisdiction in the terms and conditions.
  • A physical address (even a registered office counts).
  • How long the brand has operated. New brands are not automatically bad, but a multi-year track record is worth a lot.

If the "About us" page is empty and the terms mention no company at all, treat that as a red flag.

Step 3: Read the Withdrawal Terms

Before depositing a single peso, find the withdrawal policy and check:

  • Minimum and maximum withdrawal. Absurdly low monthly caps (or no stated cap at all) are warning signs.
  • Processing times. "Up to 5 business days" for cards is normal; "up to 30 days" for anything is not.
  • Fees. Legitimate casinos rarely charge for withdrawals; check anyway.
  • Bonus traps. If you take a bonus, the wagering requirement and max-bet rule apply to your withdrawals. Read our bonuses guide first.

Step 4: Test Customer Support

Open live chat before registering and ask two questions: "What license do you hold?" and "What is the minimum withdrawal by SPEI?" Judge by the answers:

  • Fast, specific answers in Spanish: good sign.
  • Copy-pasted scripts that dodge the question: bad sign.
  • No live chat at all, only a web form: weak, but not disqualifying if everything else checks out.

Step 5: Check the Complaint Record

Search for the casino's name plus "complaint", "not paying", or "estafa". Every large casino has some complaints - what matters is the pattern:

  • Isolated complaints that got resolved: normal.
  • Repeated "refused withdrawal" or "confiscated winnings" stories: avoid.
  • No presence anywhere - no reviews, no forum mentions: the casino may be too new to trust with real money.

Red Flags: Walk Away Immediately

  • No license information anywhere on the site.
  • Bonus offers that look impossible (for example, huge amounts with no terms linked).
  • Pressure tactics: countdown timers pushing you to deposit "now".
  • Terms and conditions missing, or only in a language you cannot read.
  • The site asks for your bank password or card PIN (a real casino never does).
  • Withdrawal requires you to deposit more first.

Protect Yourself After Registering

  • Do KYC immediately. Upload your INE or passport and proof of address before depositing.
  • Use a unique password and enable two-factor authentication if offered.
  • Keep records. Screenshot your balance, bonus terms, and chat transcripts. If a dispute ever happens, this evidence is everything. See our complaints guide.
  • Set deposit limits on day one, not after a losing streak.

FAQ

How do I verify a casino's license?

Find the license name and number in the casino's footer, then check it against the regulator's public register. For MGA licenses use the MGA website register; for Curacao check the Gaming Control Board portal. If the number is missing, fake, or unverifiable, walk away.

Is a SEGOB permit required for online casinos in Mexico?

SEGOB issues permits for gambling operations in Mexico, but there is no dedicated online casino licensing track - see our legal guide. Most sites serving Mexican players hold foreign licenses instead. What matters is that the license is real and verifiable, wherever it comes from.

What are red flags of a scam casino?

No license information, no physical company address, bonuses that look too good to be true, no clear withdrawal terms, live chat that never answers, and pressure to deposit quickly. A legitimate casino never rushes you.

Should I complete KYC before depositing?

Yes. Upload your ID and proof of address right after registering, before you deposit. If the casino has problems with your documents, you want to find out while your money is still in your bank, not after you have won.

What if a casino refuses to pay my winnings?

Document everything: screenshots of your balance, the terms you agreed to, and all chat transcripts. First complain to the casino in writing and get a ticket number. If that fails, escalate to the licensing authority or an ADR service. Our complaints guide walks through the process.
