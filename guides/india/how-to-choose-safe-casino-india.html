How to Check If an Online Casino Is Safe in India (2026)

By Spinora Wins Editorial Team | Last updated: September 2026

  1. Home
  2. India
  3. Safety Checklist
TL;DR: No domestic online casino license exists in India since the PROGA Act 2025, so safety means verifying an offshore license on the regulator's own register (MGA or Curacao GCB), checking the payout record, completing KYC before depositing, and reading the bonus terms. A logo on the casino's own site proves nothing.

Step 1: Verify the License on the Regulator's Own Register

Since no Indian online casino license exists, every casino open to Indian players is licensed offshore. The two regulators you will actually encounter:

  1. Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) - search the operator's company name in the MGA's public licensee register.
  2. Curacao Gaming Control Board (GCB) - check the operator on the GCB's verification portal.

Do this before creating an account. It takes two minutes and is the single strongest protection available. Ignore the license seal in the casino's footer until the regulator's own database confirms it.

One hard rule: any site claiming an "Indian casino license" or "Government of India license" for online casino games is lying. No such license exists. Walk away.

Step 2: Check the Payout Record

A license is the floor, not the ceiling. A licensed casino can still be slow, evasive, or hostile at payout time. Look for:

  • How long the casino has operated (years beat months).
  • Public complaint histories and how the casino responded to them.
  • Stated withdrawal timeframes per method - and whether players report they are met.
  • Clear KYC requirements published before you deposit, not invented at withdrawal time.

Our methodology explains how we score operators on exactly these factors.

Step 3: Complete KYC Before Your First Deposit

Legitimate casinos verify identity: a photo ID and proof of address, sometimes a selfie. Do this before depositing, not after winning. A casino that lets you deposit freely and only mentions verification when you try to withdraw is waving a red flag.

Use your real details. Fake names or mismatched payment accounts give the casino a legitimate reason to confiscate winnings.

Step 4: Read the Terms in Plain English

Before depositing, find and read:

  • Withdrawal rules, limits, and timeframes.
  • Bonus wagering requirements (see our bonuses guide).
  • Account dormancy and closure rules.
  • Which payment methods support withdrawals, not just deposits.

Step 5: Check Responsible-Gambling Tools

A serious operator offers deposit limits, session reminders, and self-exclusion. If none exist, the operator is cutting corners on player protection - and probably elsewhere too.

Red Flags: Walk Away Immediately

  • Claims of an "Indian license" for online casino. It does not exist.
  • No verifiable offshore license. The regulator's register is empty or the casino won't name its licensor.
  • Pressure to deposit via gift cards, P2P transfers, or unknown agents. Legitimate casinos use UPI, NetBanking, cards, e-wallets, and crypto - not informal rails.
  • No KYC. A casino that pays large withdrawals with zero verification is not compliant with anything.
  • Bonus terms you cannot find. If wagering requirements are hidden or support cannot explain them, leave.
  • Guaranteed wins or "risk-free" promises. Nobody can guarantee casino outcomes.

If Something Goes Wrong

At a licensed offshore casino: complain to the operator in writing first (email or live chat transcript), keep records, and if unresolved, escalate to the licensing regulator. Both the MGA and Curacao's GCB accept player complaints against their licensees. Our complaints guide walks through the process.

Understand the limit: no Indian regulator can force an offshore casino to pay you. This is the real cost of playing offshore, and the main reason to stick to licensed operators with real payout records.

FAQ

How do I verify an offshore casino's license?

Go to the regulator's own website - the MGA register or Curacao's GCB portal - and search for the operator's company name or license number. A license claim is only real if the regulator's own database confirms it. A logo on the casino's site proves nothing.

Is there an Indian license I should look for?

No. Since the PROGA Act 2025, no domestic online casino license exists in India. Any site claiming an "Indian casino license" for online casino games is lying. Look for recognized offshore licenses instead, such as the MGA or Curacao GCB.

What are the red flags of an unsafe casino?

No verifiable license, no KYC before withdrawals, pressure to deposit via gift cards or P2P transfers, bonus terms you cannot find, no responsible-gambling tools, and support that cannot answer basic questions about licensing.

What should I do before my first deposit?

Verify the license on the regulator's own register, complete KYC identity verification, check the withdrawal options and minimums, read the bonus terms including wagering requirements, and set deposit limits before playing.

Where can I complain if a casino refuses to pay?

First complain to the casino in writing and keep records. If unresolved, escalate to the casino's licensing regulator - the MGA and Curacao GCB both accept player complaints. No Indian regulator can force an offshore casino to pay. See our complaints guide.
