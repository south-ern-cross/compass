How Nigerian Players Can Check If a Casino Is Safe

By Spinora Wins Editorial Team | Last updated: September 2026

  1. Home
  2. Nigeria
  3. Safety Checklist
TL;DR: Verify the license on the regulator's own register, check that the company behind the casino is real, read the bonus terms before depositing, and always test with a small deposit and withdrawal first. Any casino that fails these checks does not get your money.

Step 1: Verify the License

A license is only worth something if you can verify it. Here is how:

  • Nigerian state license: look for a license statement naming a state regulator, ideally the Lagos State Lotteries and Gaming Authority (LSLGA). Contact the regulator directly to confirm the operator is on their books.
  • Offshore license: most international casinos serving Nigeria hold a Curacao or Malta license. Verify it on the regulator's public register: the Curacao Gaming Control Board or the Malta Gaming Authority.
  • Red flag: a casino that claims to be licensed but names no regulator, shows no license number, or links to a dead verification page. Treat unverifiable claims as no license at all.

Remember: since the November 2024 Supreme Court ruling, no Nigerian federal license exists for the states. Any casino still advertising an "NLRC license" as current is either outdated or dishonest. See our Nigeria legal guide for the full background.

Step 2: Check the Company Behind the Casino

Legitimate casinos name their operating company, registered address, and contact details, usually in the footer or on an "About" page. Search the company name: does it exist? Does it operate other known brands? Anonymous casinos with no company information are a major red flag.

Step 3: Read the Terms Before You Deposit

Boring but essential. Check:

  • Withdrawal limits and times - are they reasonable?
  • Bonus wagering - what is the multiplier, and does it apply to deposit plus bonus?
  • Restricted games - some bonuses exclude certain games or cap winnings.
  • KYC requirements - what documents will they ask for before paying?

If there is no terms page at all, do not deposit. Full stop.

Step 4: Test Support

Open live chat before depositing and ask a real question, for example about withdrawal times or bonus terms. Note how long they take and whether the answer is specific or copy-pasted. A casino whose support cannot answer basic questions before you deposit will not help you after.

Step 5: The Small Deposit Test

This is the single most important check, and it is how we test casinos for our reviews:

  1. Deposit the minimum amount.
  2. Play a few rounds.
  3. Request a withdrawal.
  4. Time how long it takes and note every step.

If the money arrives without excuses, the casino passes. If withdrawal is delayed, stalled, or refused without a clear reason, you have your answer - and you only risked the minimum.

Red Flags: Walk Away Immediately

  • No verifiable license, or a license from a regulator that does not exist.
  • Bonuses that look too good to be true (they usually are, or the terms make them worthless).
  • No company name, no address, no contact details.
  • Support that never responds or only sends bot replies.
  • Pressure tactics: countdown timers pushing you to deposit "now", or agents messaging you on WhatsApp.
  • Asking for your bank login details or crypto private keys. No legitimate casino ever does this.

If Something Goes Wrong

First, finish any pending KYC and re-read the terms to confirm you did not breach a bonus rule. If the casino still will not pay without a valid reason, file a complaint with us and report the operator to its licensing regulator. Keep screenshots of everything: chats, terms, transaction records.

FAQ

How do I verify a casino license in Nigeria?

Check the casino's footer for a license statement, then verify it: for Lagos licenses contact the Lagos State Lotteries and Gaming Authority, for offshore licenses use the regulator's public register (Curacao GCB, Malta MGA). A license claim you cannot verify is a red flag.

What are the red flags of a scam casino?

No verifiable license, no company name or address, bonuses that look too good to be true, no terms and conditions page, support that never answers, and pressure to deposit quickly. Any one of these is reason to walk away.

Should I test a casino with a small deposit first?

Yes. Deposit the minimum, play a little, then withdraw. If the withdrawal arrives without drama, the casino passes the most important test. Never deposit a large amount at an untested casino.

Are offshore casinos safe for Nigerians?

Licensed offshore casinos can be safe, but you get no Nigerian consumer protection if something goes wrong. Stick to well-reviewed operators with a verifiable license and a history of paying players.

What should I do if a casino refuses to pay?

First, complete any pending KYC verification and re-read the bonus terms to make sure you did not breach them. If the casino still refuses without a valid reason, file a complaint through our complaints page and report the license to the issuing regulator.
