Is Online Gambling Legal in South Africa? 2026 Guide

By Spinora Wins Editorial Team | Last updated: September 2026

  1. Home
  2. South Africa
  3. Legal Guide
TL;DR: The National Gambling Act 2004 bans interactive games - slots, roulette, poker - including from offshore sites. Online sports betting and horse racing are legal with a provincially licensed bookmaker. The NGB is the national regulator, licensing happens in the provinces, and a new comprehensive gambling bill is being developed.

The National Gambling Act 2004

South Africa's gambling framework is the National Gambling Act 7 of 2004, which replaced the 1996 legislation. The Act created the National Gambling Board (NGB) and set national norms and standards, while licensing itself happens at provincial level through nine Provincial Licensing Authorities (PLAs).

Section 11 of the Act is the crucial part: it prohibits interactive games. In South African law, "interactive games" means casino-style games played online: slots, roulette, blackjack, poker, bingo. The law was written to cover games played over the internet, and it applies to all operators regardless of where their servers sit.

The Big Split: Betting vs Interactive Games

South African law draws a hard line between two things:

  • Betting (legal with a license): placing a wager on a real-world contingency - a sports match, a horse race - through a bookmaker that holds a provincial bookmaker license. Online sports betting and online horse race betting are lawful when the bookmaker is licensed in a province.
  • Interactive games (prohibited): casino games of pure chance played online. No domestic online casino license has ever been issued under the 2004 Act.

The NGB's own FAQ is blunt: asked whether online gambling is legal in South Africa, it answers "No". Asked whether online betting is legal, it answers "Yes, with a licensed South African bookmaker."

The 2010 Ruling: Offshore Sites Are Illegal Too

On 20 August 2010 the North Gauteng High Court ruled that online gambling offered through servers located outside South Africa is illegal as well. This closed the "our servers are in Malta" argument: both offering gambling services online and gambling online became illegal, with the only exceptions being provincially licensed horse racing and online sports betting.

Who Regulates What

The NGB oversees the system at national level: norms, standards, monitoring, enforcement coordination. Day-to-day licensing belongs to the provinces. The main PLAs players encounter:

  • Western Cape Gambling and Racing Board (WCGRB) - licenses many of the country's online bookmakers.
  • Gauteng Gambling Board (GGB) - the largest land-based market.
  • Other PLAs in Mpumalanga, KwaZulu-Natal, Limpopo, North West, Free State, Northern Cape and Eastern Cape.

In April 2026 the NGB launched a Verified Operators Portal: a public database of legally licensed operators, built with the PLAs, so consumers can check whether a site is actually licensed before depositing.

What About Players?

This is where honesty matters. The prohibition is mainly enforced against operators and illegal platforms: in August 2025 the NGB, together with PLAs and law enforcement, announced intensified action against unlicensed online gambling sites targeting South Africans, and in December 2025 it directed PLAs to act against unlawful gambling advertising, including influencer marketing.

But players are not untouched by the law. The NGB states that participating in illegal gambling is a criminal offence and can result in fines or prosecution, and that under section 16 of the Act, winnings derived from unlawful gambling activities may be declared unlawful proceeds and forfeited to the state by court order. Playing offshore also means no South African regulator to complain to if a casino refuses to pay. See our complaints guide.

A New Law Is Being Developed

The 2004 framework is over 20 years old and everyone agrees it is outdated. But reform keeps stalling:

  • The National Gambling Amendment Act of 2008, which was meant to legalize and regulate interactive gambling, was never brought into force.
  • The National Gambling Amendment Bill 2018 remains stuck in parliamentary mediation.
  • A new comprehensive bill is now being developed, with consultations underway with the provinces. It proposes structural reforms: repositioning the NGB as a direct regulator, a national register of unlawful gambling operators, and stricter advertising restrictions.

As of September 2026 no new online casino licensing regime exists. This is the reform to watch.

Winnings and Tax

Recreational gambling winnings are generally not considered income for income tax purposes in South Africa. In November 2025 National Treasury published a discussion paper proposing a national online gambling tax (comments ran until February 2026), arguing that gambling has moved into a borderless digital economy and that tax should sit at national level. FAQ

Is online gambling legal in South Africa?

Online casino games - slots, roulette, poker - are not legal under the National Gambling Act 2004, which bans interactive games. Online sports betting and horse racing are legal when offered by a bookmaker holding a provincial license.

Who regulates gambling in South Africa?

The National Gambling Board (NGB) sets national norms and standards. Actual licensing is done by nine Provincial Licensing Authorities, such as the Western Cape Gambling and Racing Board (WCGRB) and the Gauteng Gambling Board.

Can I play at offshore casinos from South Africa?

A 2010 High Court ruling made online gambling through servers outside South Africa illegal as well. The NGB warns that participating in illegal gambling is a criminal offence and that winnings from unlawful gambling can be forfeited to the state. Enforcement mainly targets operators and unlicensed platforms.

Is a new gambling law coming?

A comprehensive new gambling bill is being developed with consultations underway; the 2018 Amendment Bill remains stuck in parliamentary mediation. In April 2026 the NGB launched a public Verified Operators Portal listing licensed operators.

Do I pay tax on gambling winnings?

Recreational gambling winnings are generally not treated as income for tax purposes. In November 2025 National Treasury published a discussion paper proposing a national online gambling tax (comments ran until February 2026), but it is a proposal, not law. We are not tax advisors: speak to a South African tax professional about large winnings.

How do I verify a licensed operator?

Check the NGB's Verified Operators Portal and the website of the relevant Provincial Licensing Authority. A licensed bookmaker displays a valid provincial license. Online casino sites hold no South African license at all.
