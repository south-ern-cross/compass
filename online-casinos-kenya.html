How It Works

For nearly six decades Kenyan gambling was governed by the Betting, Lotteries and Gaming Act, Cap. 131 of 1966, which established the Betting Control and Licensing Board (BCLB). The old law barely addressed online gambling, even as Kenya became one of Africa's largest mobile-betting markets. The Gambling Control Act 2025 changes that: it creates distinct licence categories for online gambling (online bookmakers, online casinos, online lotteries), sets a minimum online stake of 20 KES per bet, and strengthens player-protection, advertising, anti-money-laundering and responsible-gambling requirements. Online gambling licences under the new Act carry steep fees - reported at 100 million KES for a cross-county online licence - and licences run for 36 months.

The regulator is also changing. The BCLB announced in October 2025 that it was suspending all annual licence applications - new and renewals alike - to prepare the transfer of its responsibilities to the new Gambling Regulatory Authority of Kenya (GRA), with the transition expected to finish by the end of February 2026. Until the GRA is fully operational, existing licensees keep operating under their current licences and conditions. This means the legal picture is settled in principle but still settling in practice: always check an operator's current licence status with the regulator before depositing.

Player protection got tougher in 2026. The Gambling Control (Conduct of Gambling Operations) Regulations, gazetted on 30 June 2026, let family members apply to the regulator to exclude a relative whose gambling causes or risks serious financial hardship, and allow licensed operators themselves to suspend customers showing signs of compulsive or unaffordable gambling - with the regulator reviewing the decision within 24 hours. Advertising is also tightly controlled: gambling ads must be approved and carry age restrictions, with no influencer marketing. The legal gambling age in Kenya is 18.

For individual players, playing at an offshore-licensed casino is common but sits outside the local licensing net: only operators holding a Kenyan licence (BCLB legacy or new GRA licences) are formally authorized to serve Kenyan players. Winnings are subject to withholding tax under Kenyan tax law - keep records and consult a local tax professional, as rates and rules change.

Payment Methods

Kenya runs on mobile money. M-Pesa (Safaricom) is the dominant payment rail - instant deposits straight from your phone, used by every locally licensed bookmaker. Airtel Money is the main alternative. Bank cards are accepted at some operators but are far less popular than mobile money. At offshore crypto casinos, crypto (BTC, USDT and others) is the standard route: no bank blocks, payouts in minutes. Note that most international crypto casinos do not support M-Pesa directly - if M-Pesa is a must, locally licensed bookmakers are the practical option.

FAQ

Is online gambling legal in Kenya?

Yes, it is regulated. The Gambling Control Act 2025 replaced the 1966 law and introduced dedicated online-casino licences, but the regulator handover from the BCLB to the new Gambling Regulatory Authority of Kenya is still being completed (as of September 2026). Only locally licensed operators are formally authorized to serve Kenyan players; many Kenyans also play at offshore-licensed casinos.

What is the legal gambling age in Kenya?

18 years or older.

What is the BCLB / GRA?

The Betting Control and Licensing Board (BCLB) was Kenya's gambling regulator under the 1966 law. The Gambling Control Act 2025 replaces it with the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Kenya (GRA); the transition was expected to be completed by the end of February 2026.

What is the 20 KES minimum stake rule?

The Gambling Control Act 2025 sets a minimum stake of 20 Kenyan shillings per online bet, as part of its player-protection measures.
