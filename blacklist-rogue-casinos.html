How to spot a rogue casino yourself

  • Unverifiable license. Check the license on the regulator's own website, not the casino's footer. Fake license claims are one of the most common rogue tells.
  • A complaint pattern. Search "[casino name] complaints" on CasinoMeister, AskGamblers and Casino Guru. One angry review means little; dozens with the same story mean a lot.
  • Predatory bonus terms. Impossible wagering, hidden win caps, or withdrawal conditions buried in the fine print (see Grand Duke above).
  • Pirated games. Real providers (Pragmatic Play, NetEnt, Evolution) list their licensed partners. If the games look slightly off, they may be stolen copies with rigged math.
  • Support goes quiet when you win. Fast, friendly support until withdrawal time - then silence - is a classic pattern.
  • No responsible gambling tools. Legitimate casinos offer deposit limits, cool-offs and self-exclusion. Rogue ones don't bother.
  • Brand-new site, enormous bonuses. A 500% bonus from a casino nobody has heard of is not generosity; it is bait.

What to do if a casino won't pay you

  1. Document everything. Screenshot your balance, the withdrawal request, bonus terms as shown when you accepted them, and every support chat or email.
  2. Finish verification first. Complete every KYC step the casino asks for. Many "non-payment" cases are really incomplete-verification cases - eliminate that excuse.
  3. Ask in writing. Email support asking for the specific reason and a concrete timeline. Vague "under review" replies with no date are a red flag.
  4. File a mediation complaint. AskGamblers, Casino Guru and ThePOGG all run free complaint services and contact the casino directly. Public cases get resolved far more often than private emails.
  5. Post on the CasinoMeister forum. Many casinos have reps there, and public scrutiny moves cases that private tickets don't.
  6. Contact the licensing authority. If the license is real, the regulator has a complaints process. If the license turns out to be fake, you have your answer about the casino.
  7. Chargeback as a last resort. Your bank or card provider may reverse the deposit. Note this will almost certainly get your casino account closed - use it when the money matters more than the account.

FAQ

What is a rogue casino?

A rogue casino is an operator with a documented pattern of unfair behavior: refusing or stalling withdrawals, confiscating winnings on thin pretexts, using predatory bonus terms, running pirated game software, or claiming fake licenses. Watchdog sites like CasinoMeister, AskGamblers and Casino Guru track and list them.

How do I check if an online casino is legit before depositing?

Search the casino name plus "complaints" on CasinoMeister, AskGamblers and Casino Guru. Verify the license on the regulator's own site, not just the casino's footer. Check how long the casino has operated and whether it responds to public complaints.

What does it mean when a casino is blacklisted?

It means an independent watchdog reviewed player complaints and evidence and decided the casino cannot be trusted. Blacklisted casinos are removed from recommendation lists and players are warned to stay away.

Can I get my money back from a rogue casino?

Sometimes. Document everything, complete identity verification, then file a complaint with AskGamblers, Casino Guru or ThePOGG mediation services and post on the CasinoMeister forum. Public pressure resolves many cases. If the casino is truly unlicensed, recovery is harder and a bank chargeback may be the last resort.

Are all Curacao-licensed casinos rogue?

No. Many legitimate crypto casinos operate under Curacao licenses and pay reliably. The license alone is not the test - payout behavior, complaint history and responsiveness are. That said, a casino claiming a license it does not actually hold is a major red flag.
