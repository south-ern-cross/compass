Status: Regulated, but the framework is mid-reform. Online gambling in Mexico is governed by the Federal Gaming and Raffles Law (Ley Federal de Juegos y Sorteos), which dates to 1947 and was not written with online casinos in mind. The Interior Ministry (SEGOB), through its Games and Raffles Directorate (DGJS), issues permits - but there is no standalone online-only license: online operations are authorized only as an extension of an existing land-based casino or sportsbook permit. In September 2025 SEGOB head Rosa Icela Rodríguez announced a comprehensive reform of the framework; the internal review of the proposal was completed in 2026 and the draft is to be submitted to the Security Cabinet and then Congress, but no new law is in force as of September 2026. Separately, an IEPS tax reform effective 1 January 2026 created a new route: foreign operators can serve Mexican players by registering with the tax authority (SAT), paying the gaming tax and filing monthly reports - without a SEGOB permit. Most Mexican players use internationally licensed offshore operators.

How It Works

Mexico's Ley Federal de Juegos y Sorteos was enacted on 31 December 1947 and gives SEGOB exclusive authority over gaming and lottery activity (except the National Lottery). The law predates the internet entirely - a 2025 congressional transparency proposal noted that the legislation "did not consider online gaming modalities." In practice, SEGOB's DGJS issues five categories of permits (casino/gaming rooms, sports betting agencies, racetracks, lotteries/raffles, and online gaming extensions), and the online category exists only as an add-on to a land-based permit. There is no application for an internet-only gambling permit in Mexico as of 2026. Casino operations are prohibited outright in five states: Baja California Sur, Coahuila, Guanajuato, Nuevo León and Tlaxcala.

Reform has been discussed for years but keeps stalling. In September 2025, SEGOB head Rosa Icela Rodríguez announced a comprehensive overhaul aimed at money laundering and tax fraud in the sector; in 2026 she confirmed the internal review of the proposal was complete and that it would go to the Security Cabinet before a possible congressional filing. Around 20 casinos linked to money laundering or tax fraud were shut down in coordination with the Attorney General's office (FGR) and the financial intelligence unit (UIF). Industry association AIEJA has publicly complained about the lack of concrete progress, noting that since a March 2025 working session with SEGOB "there has been no concrete progress, and we don't even have sight of a draft."

The most significant practical development is fiscal, not regulatory. A reform to the IEPS (Special Tax on Production and Services) law effective 1 January 2026 pulled internet-based betting into the tax system: a foreign operator can now serve Mexican players by registering with SAT, paying the gaming tax and filing monthly reports, without needing a SEGOB permit. Gaming lawyer Alfredo Lazcano has described it as "not a traditional licence; it is another way to operate legally in the market," adding that an MGA-licensed entity can now serve Mexico legally without a physical presence as long as it meets the tax requirements. Note that a 50% IEPS excise rate applies to games with bets and draws conducted online by foreign residents without an establishment in Mexico - a cost operators must absorb or pass on.

For individual players, playing at an offshore-licensed casino is not explicitly criminalized; the legal exposure sits primarily with unlicensed operators. Still, the framework is in flux, so treat any operator's claims about its Mexican legal status with skepticism and verify against official SEGOB/SAT sources.

Payment Methods

Mexican players typically deposit via SPEI - Mexico's instant interbank transfer system, using a CLABE account number - which is the standard rail for moving pesos in and out of gaming accounts. OXXO cash vouchers let players deposit cash at any OXXO convenience store, a popular option for the unbanked. Debit/credit cards work at some operators but are frequently blocked for gambling transactions, which is why crypto (BTC, USDT and others) has become the most reliable route at offshore casinos: no bank blocks, payouts in minutes, and a hedge against peso volatility.

FAQ

Is online gambling legal in Mexico?

The framework is mid-reform. The 1947 Federal Gaming and Raffles Law requires a SEGOB permit, and there is no standalone online-only license - but playing at an offshore-licensed casino is not explicitly criminalized for individual players. Since 1 January 2026, foreign operators can also serve Mexico legally via the IEPS tax-registration route without a SEGOB permit. What is the legal gambling age in Mexico?

18 years or older.

What is SEGOB / DGJS?

SEGOB (Secretaría de Gobernación, the Interior Ministry) is Mexico's gambling regulator, acting through its Games and Raffles Directorate (DGJS), which issues gaming permits. Online operations are only authorized as an extension of a land-based permit.

What is SPEI?

SPEI is Mexico's interbank electronic payment system for instant peso transfers, identified by an 18-digit CLABE account number. It is the standard local rail for casino deposits and withdrawals.

Can I deposit with cash in Mexico?

Yes - OXXO cash vouchers let you deposit pesos in cash at any OXXO convenience store; many offshore operators support them alongside SPEI and crypto.

Do I pay tax on casino winnings in Mexico?

Winnings from games with bets can be subject to withholding under Mexican tax law, and the 2026 IEPS changes affect how operators are taxed. Rules depend on the game type and amounts - this is not tax advice; consult a local tax professional for your situation.
